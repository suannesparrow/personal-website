04 / PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT · MATERIALS · DFMA

RC vehicle
performance.

A team project combining composite material testing, chassis redesign, structural analysis, and manufacturing-process planning to reduce vehicle weight and support scalable production.

PROJECTRC vehicle modificationMY ROLEChassis and manufacturing designTIMEFRAMESeptember 2023–April 2024

THE CHALLENGE

Reduce mass without designing past the manufacturing process.

The project explored a lighter RC vehicle chassis and shell, with the design informed by material testing, structural analysis, and manufacturing constraints. The work connected composite layup choices to the part geometry and production method.

PROJECT CONCEPT · ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY

DESIGN & TESTING

Use test data to guide material choices.

  • Redesigned the chassis around glass-fiber-reinforced polymer and resin transfer molding.
  • Compared fiber orientations using tensile and bending tests to inform the composite layup.
  • Used DFMA and FEA to assess the shell design and its suitability for high-volume thermoforming.
  • Developed lightweight tires and rims using polyurethane and PVC injection molding.

OUTCOME

15% less weight, with production in view.

The modified chassis reduced weight by 15%. The shell concept was developed around a thermoforming process with a projected throughput of 83,200 units per year.

15% lower vehicle weight

Reported project result. The thermoforming throughput is a process estimate, not a claim of actual production volume.
